## Answers to the AMC-144 reading goals

### 1. How they define "symbolic" — CONFIRMED: object-centric extraction, NOT RAM reads ✅

Symbolic states are **object-centric representations extracted from the
pixel input** using the object-centric extraction module of Delfosse et
al. 2024 (OCAtari lineage): a list of n objects × m properties (x, y,
orientation, value…). The symbolic channel is *vision-derived*, not
privileged emulator state. Our symbolic treatment reads ground-truth
RAM (position, party stats, event flags) — a different, stronger
information condition.

### 2. How neural and symbolic combine — policy blending, NOT observation concatenation ✅

BlendRL is a **mixture of two separate policies**, not one policy over
a combined observation:

- Neural policy π_neural: CNN over pixel frames → action distribution.
- Logic policy π_logic: differentiable forward reasoner over
  first-order-logic action rules on the object-centric state. Rules,
  predicates, and valuation functions are **generated by an LLM
  (GPT-4o)** from a task description, with learnable rule weights.
- A learned blending module outputs β ∈ [0,1];
  final policy = β·π_neural(x) + (1−β)·π_logic(z) (their Eq. 1),
  plus an entropy-style regularizer on β so neither module dies.
- Trained end-to-end with PPO actor-critic; hybrid value function
  V = β·V_CNN(x) + (1−β)·V_MLP(z) (Eq. 2).

### 3. Environments and scale

Three Atari games chosen for reasoning+reacting structure: **Kangaroo,
Seaquest, Donkey Kong** (ALE + HackAtari variants for robustness
tests). Trained to convergence (15–25k episodes); robustness table
over 10 seeds, mean ± std. Short-horizon reactive tasks — nothing
like a 15k-step story-driven POMDP.

### 4. What they compare and what wins

BlendRL vs pure-neural (DQN, pixel-CNN PPO) and logic/neuro-symbolic
agents (NLRL, NUDGE, SCoBots, INTERPRETER, INSIGHT). BlendRL wins on
human-normalized score everywhere (e.g. Kangaroo 474 vs PPO 26) and is
the only agent that survives HackAtari environment modifications
(neural PPO drops to 0.0). **Important nuance:** their "symbolic
baselines" are *different algorithm families* (logic-rule policies),
not the same PPO fed symbolic observations — it is an architecture
comparison, not a controlled representation comparison.

### 5. Encoder capacity matching — CONFIRMED ABSENT ✅

No parameter or FLOP matching anywhere. The neural and logic modules
are fundamentally different parameterizations (CNN vs weighted FOL
rules), so a capacity-controlled comparison is not even expressible in
their framework. Confounds our study controls (encoder capacity,
per-condition LR) are uncontrolled here — which is fine for their
claim (the *framework* helps) but means BlendRL cannot answer "which
representation is better, all else equal."

## Differentiation paragraph (draft for related work)

> BlendRL (Shindo et al., 2025) demonstrates that blending a pixel-CNN
> policy with an LLM-generated logic policy over object-centric states
> outperforms either alone on reactive Atari tasks. BlendRL varies the
> policy class and the representation simultaneously: its symbolic
> stream is object-centric state extracted from pixels, its symbolic
> policy is a differentiable rule reasoner, and no attempt is made to
> equalize capacity across modules. Our study asks the complementary
> controlled question: holding the algorithm, policy architecture, and
> reward fixed, and matching encoder parameter count across
> conditions, does a pixel, RAM-derived symbolic, or hybrid
> observation drive better learning in a long-horizon POMDP? Where
> BlendRL's hybrid wins by arbitrating between two policies, our
> hybrid fuses modalities inside a single recurrent policy —
> isolating representation, not architecture, as the factor.

## Risk assessment for our novelty claim

BlendRL's headline ("hybrid beats pure pixel and pure symbolic") sounds
adjacent to our H3, so the related-work section must draw the three
distinctions explicitly: (1) policy blending vs observation fusion,
(2) vision-derived object-centric vs privileged RAM symbolic,
(3) uncontrolled vs capacity-matched comparison. With those stated,
no preemption: **the claim "no prior pixel-vs-symbolic comparison has
matched encoder parameter count and FLOPs across conditions" survives
this read.**
